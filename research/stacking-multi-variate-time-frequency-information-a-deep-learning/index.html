Stacking Multi-Variate Time–Frequency Information: A Deep Learning Approach for IMU–MMG Based Hand Gesture Recognition

Sadman Rahman, Md. Mehedi Hasan Bhuiyan, Nasiruddin Mahmud Himel, Rishi Raj Sharma, Kazi Newaj Faisal

publishedIEEE Sensors Journal
Read the paper ↗All research

Abstract

Natural and reliable hand gesture recognition is becoming a key enabler for intuitive human-computer interaction in internet of things (IoT) environments, especially for wearable and assistive systems where camera-based sensing is unsuitable. This paper presents an inertial measurement unit (IMU) based framework that converts raw tri-axial accelerometer and gyroscope signals into structured time-frequency images and classifies them using a lightweight convolutional network. First, we propose a stackingbased multivariate time-frequency representation method termed TFR-StackGrid. In this method, the smoothed pseudo Wigner-Ville distribution (SPWVD) is computed for each of the six IMU-MMG channels. The resulting time-frequency maps are then systematically arranged into a fixed 2×3 kinematic grid. This structured configuration preserves the spatial relationship between translational acceleration and rotational motion, thereby integrating multivariate information into a single stacked time-frequency representation, referred to as S-TFR. A compact Gesture Net architecture is then designed to operate directly on these images, combining stacked 2D convolution blocks with a shallow classification head for 11 dynamic hand gesture classes. We conduct a comprehensive evaluation on the large-scale hand gesture accelerometer and gyroscope Dataset (HGAG-DATA), containing 11 gestures, 23,650 samples from 43 participants, and compare seven different time-frequency schemes, including wavelet and other quadratic distributions. The proposed SPWVD-based TFR-StackGrid with Gesture Net achieves 96.21% micro accuracy and 96.28% macro accuracy, with balanced per-class precision, recall, and F1scores, while relying only on a single wrist-worn IMU based mechanomyography signals. These results demonstrate that structured time-frequency imaging combined with a lightweight convolutional backbone can effectively mitigate the limitations of template matching, handcrafted feature pipelines, and heavier deep sequence models for practical IMU-MMG-based hand gesture recognition.
