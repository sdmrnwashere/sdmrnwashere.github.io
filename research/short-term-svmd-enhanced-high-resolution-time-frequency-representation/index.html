Short-Term SVMD-Enhanced High-Resolution Time–Frequency Representation for ECG Signal Analysis

Sadman Rahman, Kazi Newaj Faisal

acceptedIEEE SPMB
Link coming soonAll research

Abstract

Accurate time-frequency (TF) analysis of the electrocardiogram (ECG) is vital for interpreting its beat-to-beat activity, yet its recurring waveforms make crossterm-free representation difficult. The Wigner–Ville distribution (WVD) offers the highest TF resolution among bilinear representations, but its quadratic nature generates inner and outer cross-terms that obscure the true energy of nonlinear and multi-component non-stationary signals. Variational mode decomposition (VMD) based methods reduce these terms but require the number of modes in advance and are sensitive to initialization, while whole-signal successive VMD (SVMD) cannot separate components that share a narrow band at different time instants. This paper proposes a short-term SVMD based WVD (ST-SVMD-WVD) that segments the signal with overlapping short-term windows, applies SVMD to each segment to obtain automatically selected band-limited modes, and combines their WVDs with an overlap-compensating energy-scaling step. The short-term segmentation isolates same-band components occurring at different times and linearizes nonlinear instantaneous-frequency laws within each window, while SVMD removes the need to preset the number of modes and is robust to initialization. The method is tested on two synthetic signals, a normal sinus rhythm ECG under clean and noisy conditions (5, 10, and 15 dB), and four arrhythmia classes from the MIT-BIH database, and is compared with five reference methods using four TF measures. ST-SVMD-WVD gives the best result on all four measures for both synthetic signals. On the ECG signals it gives the lowest normalized Rényi entropy and highest energy concentration in all normal-rhythm cases and in three of the four arrhythmia classes. Some reference methods score better on the Stankovi´c measure or ratio of norms in some normal-rhythm cases, but their TFRs show scattered or blurred components, whereas ST-SVMD-WVD shows one compact component at each beat on a clean background.

In one line

A short-term, SVMD-based time–frequency representation that gives sharper views of ECG signals.
