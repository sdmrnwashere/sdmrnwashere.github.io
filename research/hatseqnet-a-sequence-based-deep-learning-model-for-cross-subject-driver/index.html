HatSeqNet: A Sequence-Based Deep Learning Model for Cross-Subject Driver Drowsiness Detection Using Single-Channel EEG Signal

Sadman Rahman, Saquib Farhan, Rishi Raj Sharma, Kazi Newaj Faisal

publishedIEEE Sensors Journal
Read the paper ↗All research

Abstract

Driver drowsiness represents a significant risk to road safety, being a major contributor to traffic accidents. This study introduces a hybrid adaptive temporal sequential network HatSeqNet, an effectively designed architecture for cross-subject driver drowsiness detection using single-channel electroencephalogram (EEG) signals across different subjects. Leveraging the Mexican hat wavelet (MHW) transform, EEG signals are transformed into time-scale representations (TSRs), capturing intricate neural patterns and transient dynamics. These TSRs are processed by a hybrid convolutional neural network (CNN) and recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture, which effectively models spatial and temporal dependencies. The model is evaluated using leave-one-subject-out cross-validation (LOSO-CV) on a publicly available EEG dataset from a sustained-attention driving task. HatSeqNet achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 88.26%, with precision, recall, and F1-score of 88.87%, 87.63%, and 88.10%, respectively. These metrics surpass those of prior studies on the same SAT dataset. The employment of single-channel EEG enhances the system’s portability, rendering HatSeqNet suitable for practical vehicular applications. The superior performance of the proposed architecture underscores its potential to enhance road safety through reliable and noninvasive driver drowsiness detection, even without personalized calibration.

In one line

A sequence model that detects driver drowsiness in unseen subjects from a single EEG channel.
